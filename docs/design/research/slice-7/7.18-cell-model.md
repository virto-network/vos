# 7.18x: the CPU split, a cell and time model

The exploration before step 7.18 (`slice-7-mobile.md`, "The CPU split by
opcode family"): what each family's component would hold, counted from the
CPU chip's own constraints; what the split does to the committed cells and
the phone's time on the auth device kernel, on heavier kernels and on the
node's segments; the first-day count of the node's kernel leaves (S32); and
the cheaper variants the counts point to. Nothing in the library changed.
The model is `spikes/s7-split/` (`model.py`, its output
`runs/model-out.txt`); every table below is that output's, rounded.

Tags as in the plan: [measured] by a run, [model] by the script over the
AIR's own expressions and the traced runs, [est] arithmetic on top.

## The answer

- **The plan's 4-way split pays little where the device is today.** On the
  auth kernel (1,722 steps) it saves 5.4% of the cells (607 → about 570 ms
  on the phone); on node segments 1–8%. Its families repeat a 109-column
  core and their per-row lookups, so it adds width: 785 columns become
  1,284. It saves CPU cells only where the families' row counts fall below
  the CPU's padded height. At an exact power of two of steps (from 2^12)
  it changes the CPU's cells by +1% to −8%; just past one, it saves
  42–54%. Averaged over kernel lengths it saves 33–47% of the CPU's cells
  from 2^11 steps on, below the plan's −50–70%. On node segments, at a
  class's fixed shape, it saves under 1%.
- **What the data argue for is a per-program CPU:** one component whose
  columns follow the program's code, every column only absent opcodes need
  left out, as the chip set already leaves out absent chips. Every kernel
  traced uses 48–55 of the 139 opcodes and no division, bit count, rotation,
  sign extension, min/max or signed MulUpper. The CPU loses 29–32% of its
  columns (785 → 535–549) at every length: no floors, no linking, no new
  component. The auth kernel's cells drop 8.8% (about 554 ms), heavier
  kernels' 14–17%. The node's leaves get narrower, not wider.
- **The best split is two-way (ALU | the rest) on top of the per-program
  CPU:** −14.6% of the auth kernel's cells (about 518 ms), −48–53% of the
  CPU's cells averaged over lengths above 2^10 steps. Its gain over the
  per-program CPU alone comes from padding: at the auth kernel its two
  components hold 874 and 848 of their 1,024 rows, and at an exact power
  of two of steps it gains nothing over the per-program CPU (window at
  2^14: −12.8% against −14.5%). The 4-way split over the per-program CPU
  saves 5–8 points more of the CPU's cells from 2^12 steps on (2–3% of a
  kernel's cells), less at the auth size. With the Poseidon2 chip it fits
  a node leaf by under 1%, inside the model's error. Over the full ISA it
  overflows the leaf.
- **Word-level memory (7.19) beats any split on the auth kernel:** −17%,
  because its memory ledger is byte-level at 2^14 rows for 1,722 steps.
  With the per-program CPU, −26% (about 450 ms).
- **First-day count (S32):** no node kernel leaf passes 2^16 `p2` rows at
  40 queries under any split for today's node kernels (the worst, 5-way,
  62,070). With the Poseidon2 chip in the shape (a node kernel calling
  `0x402`, which format 41 exists for), the 4- and 5-way splits pass it
  (66,254 and 67,928). Under S32's rule the 4-way split is device-only. The
  per-program CPU narrows every leaf (57,745 → 56,424; 62,322 with the
  chip). A 2-way split with the chip fits by 920 rows, less than the byte
  ledger growing from 2^18 to 2^19 rows would add (+1,042): it fits only
  for kernels whose classes keep the ledger at 2^18.
- **Heavier circuits: time binds first under PG2's budgets.** On the phone
  1.0 s is about 20M committed cells. 1 GiB of peak is 26–40M: 26M by the
  fit through P5's BLAKE2b-statement kernel (another shape, format 43),
  40M by P6's three format-45 points alone. So a device kernel of node-like
  code fits 1 s up to about 2^11–2^12 steps, and memory binds only if the
  time budget is relaxed. None of the CPU levers moves either limit by more
  than 8–24% of the cells. A kernel of 2^14 steps needs about 3 s and
  1.6–2.2 GiB; one of 2^16, 9–11 s or more and several GiB. One heavier
  kernel measured at format 45 would pin the peak's slope.

## Method

**The families' columns, from the AIR itself** [model]. A test in the CPU
chip's module (`dump.patch`, not committed) runs each of the CPU's 57
constraint functions through stwo's `ExprEvaluator` and writes every
constraint as an expression over the main columns, every relation entry
(relation, multiplicity, the tuple's columns), and every opcode's 80
program-memory flags (`classify_opcode_for_program_memory`). The dump has
776 constraints and 99 relation entries over 581 main columns: format 41's
and 45's `CpuP2Chip` exactly (format 35's `CpuChip` is the same less the
Poseidon2 selector, 9 constraints and 3 entries).

`cpu_families.py` reduces the AIR to a component that holds only some
opcodes' rows:

1. Every flag column no held opcode sets is zero, and so is every host-call
   selector whose call the component does not hold.
2. The zeros are propagated, decided by evaluating at random points over
   M31. A constraint left linear in one column, vanishing at zero, zeroes
   that column (a helper `h − flag·x`). A sum of booleans with positive
   coefficients left equal to zero zeroes them (`MemSize = Σ active`). A
   flag byte whose bits are all zero is zero. A constraint left with no
   column is dropped, and so is an entry whose multiplicity vanishes or a
   table lookup of constants.
3. Liveness is a least fixpoint from the relations to other chips. A
   constraint linear, with a constant coefficient, in a column it alone
   defines is that column's definition. Other constraints over two or more
   columns are checks, and their columns are live. Table lookups (range,
   nibble, flag bits) live with their columns.
4. Ten witness groups, listed in `GROUPS`, are computed on every row today
   whatever the opcode. They are ValD's and the remainder's zero tests, the
   MSB chains, the quotient and remainder with their signs, the operand
   signs, a 32-bit result's sign, a signed load's sign, truncation,
   equality and less-than, and the host calls' slots. A component none of
   whose opcodes consumes a group drops it with every entry touching it.
   This step is by hand, from the constraint code: each group names the
   flags of its consumers.

Run on the full ISA as one component, the reduction keeps 573 of the 581
columns. Seven `MemByteActiveMonoH` columns are pinned to zero in today's
AIR (`active_bytes` constrains each to 0 and defines it as the product it
stands for). One `IsLoadLocalNotActiveH` limb is read by nothing. Both are
small free cuts. (The group rule is coarse in the safe direction: some
groups are kept where a finer predicate would drop them, as ValD's zero
test for code whose only compares are `SetLt`.)

**The instruction mixes** [measured]: a hook in the kernel suite's
`traced` (also in `dump.patch`) writes each traced kernel's opcode
histogram. It writes them for every 2^15-step chunk and for the 2^12–2^16
prefixes, with the segment AIR's natural log sizes per window
(`canonical_profile_for_bounds`), the bytes the run loads, stores and is
handed, the distinct bytes it touches, and the code's static opcodes. The
kernels are the device auth kernel (1,722 steps) and the node kernels
`balance` (21,733), `window` (52,178), and `accounts` (35,465–248,924).

**The device shape at format 45** [measured, `x86-h256.jsonl`]: the auth
kernel's 19 chips, their columns and log sizes, 11.67M cells. For a heavier
kernel the device chips' sizes come from its run. The memory ledger takes a
row per accessed byte and two per byte first touched and closed. The device
boundary takes a row per distinct byte. The shared chips take the segment
AIR's natural sizes. Every chip is held at its 2^10 floor at least. This
reproduces the auth kernel's measured sizes exactly. The data table is held
at the auth kernel's 2^13, and the Poseidon2 chip at its floor, since node
kernels hash with 15–65 BLAKE2b calls per 2^14–2^16 steps. Program memory is
the node kernels' 2^16, which is pessimistic for a purpose-built device
kernel.

**A component's columns** are its main columns, plus four interaction
columns per pair of entries (the CPU pairs its fractions), plus four
blinding columns when masked. Its rows are its family's steps, padded to a
power of two at the floor. Today's CPU is 581 + 200 + 4 = 785 columns,
exactly as measured.

**The leaf** [model]: `leaf.py` ports `vos_pvm_compiler::leaf` (the
derivation's bound; its chip tables are parsed from `leaf.rs`). The port
gives the window kernel's class-1 leaf exactly as the Rust test does:
57,700 / 102,173 / 46,000 rows, 16 points, 1,150 blocks, 785 singles.
`count-35.log`'s 59,300 is the older count. The built circuit's measured
terms (`recursion/leaves.rs`) are `leaf.rs`'s. A family component enters
the leaf as a chip with its main and interaction columns and the relations
it draws. Its gates are scaled from the CPU's 2,850 for 815 constraints,
which moves `qm31` only. Program memory gains one preprocessed column, the
family id its lookup must carry.

**The shared core:** 109 main columns every family keeps. They are the
timestamp, the next timestamp and its carries (23); `pc`, `next_pc` and
its carries, the opcode and the skip (13); the register indices and the
operand and result slots `ValB`, `ValD` and `Result` (27); `RegValB` and
`RegValD` (16); the immediates and the branch target (17); the register
access selectors and their helpers (10); padding; and two flag bytes. The
per-row relations each family repeats are 24–30 entries, 48–60
interaction columns. They are the result's and the next timestamp's byte
range checks (16), program execution (2), program memory (2), register
reads and writes (3–8), and the flag bits (1–5).

**Linking costs nothing new.** The program-execution relation already
chains steps as a multiset over `(ts, pc) → (ts + 1, next_pc)`, so rows may
sit in any component. A step's terminality rests, today as after a split,
on the timestamp chain and that multiset, anchored by the boundary's final
`(ts, pc)`: nothing makes padding a suffix. Four constraints read the next
row's `IsPadding` and are restated per component: the trap
(`control.rs`), the halts through `JumpInd` and `LoadImmJumpInd`
(`control.rs`), and an unacknowledged host call (`host_call.rs`). So both
control and host components keep the next-row read; the counts give every
component that column. A family id in the program-memory tuple (a constant
per component, one preprocessed column in program memory) is cheap
insurance rather than a necessity: the flag bytes already pin a row to its
opcode's canonical flags, and another family's flags are constant zero, so
only an opcode with no flags (`Fallthrough`, harmlessly) could cross. The
logup cost is the repetition above, each component paying its own per-row
entries.

## 3. Time against cells

[measured points, fits in `model.py`]

- **Phone** (Fairphone 6, `Auto`, the four big cores). The points are P6's
  three cells (11.67M → 607 ms, 15.92M → 741, 19.69M → 952) and P5's
  BLAKE2b-statement kernel (44.9M → 2,430). The affine fit is
  `−117 + 56.4 ms per M cells`; the proportional one 51.3 ms per M. Their
  residuals are within 11% (the affine reads the smallest cell 11% low, the
  proportional the 44.9M cell 5% low). The per-cell rates (52, 46.5, 48,
  54 ms per M) are not monotone: time is linear in committed cells over
  11.7–45M within about 10%. Projections inside the range scale a measured
  anchor by the cells; beyond 45M they are extrapolations and a lower
  bound (FFTs at 2^16–2^19 rows cost `n log n`).
- **Phone peak:** `22 + 38.8 MiB per M cells` through all four points,
  1 GiB ≈ 25.8M cells; its slope comes from P5's 44.9M cell, another shape
  under format 43. P6's three format-45 points alone give
  `367 + 16.4 MiB per M`, 1 GiB ≈ 40M cells. So **1 GiB is 26–40M cells**,
  past 1.0 s's 20M either way: under PG2's budgets time binds first.
- **x86** (4 P-cores, 7.17c's session): `118 + 18.0 ms per M cells`. The
  phone over x86 is 1.85× at 11.7M and 2.02× at 19.7M.
- **What cells miss.** The split also moves constraint evaluation (23% of a
  proof on x86, 7.17c's breakdown). The auth kernel's CPU evaluates 826
  constraints on 2^12 points; the 4-way components evaluate 1,114 in total
  on 2^11 each, so the CPU's constraint work falls about 33% against its
  cells' 20%: a little more gain than the table shows. Against that, each
  component brings fixed costs the cells do not count: a claimed sum, R1's
  per-column check, its trace pass. They are small against a 600 ms proof
  [est], but they decide the sign near the crossover.

## 4. The crossover

The CPU's cells under each variant against today's (785 columns at twice
the padded steps), for the auth mix and the auth kernel's code. The node
mix and code are within a few points (per program −30%, 2-way per program
−48…−51%, 4-way −12…−43%).

the mean over 64 log-spaced lengths between them.

- **Up to 2^10 steps no split pays:** every family sits at the 2^10 floor,
  and the split costs 22–100% of the CPU's cells (+50–83 ms on the phone
  for 4- and 5-way). Only the per-program CPU pays, −32%.
- **The 2-way split pays from just past 2^10 steps** (−38% on average; at
  the auth kernel's 1,722, −39%, about −65 ms). The 4-way split pays on
  average from the same octave (−18%) but passes the 2-way's mean only
  from 2^12 steps on, and at an exact power of two it changes the CPU's
  cells by +1% to −8%.
- **In time:** the phone pays about 51 ms per million cells. A 2^14-step
  kernel's CPU is 25.7M cells, so −45% of it is about 0.6 s of a 3 s proof.
  For the auth kernel the best variant (2-way per program) saves 1.7M
  cells, about 90 ms.
- The plan's −50–70% (`proving.md` §9) is reached only averaged over
  lengths, from 2^12–2^13 steps on, by the 5-way split or by a split over
  the per-program CPU.

## 5. Alternatives at the heavier sizes [model]

| kernel | today | per-program CPU | word-level memory (7.19) | right shifts through MUL | per-program + word memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| auth, 1,722 steps | 11.67M | −9% | −17% | −7% | **−26%** |
| window, 16,384 | 56.2M | −14% | −13…−15% | −2% | −27…−30% |
| balance, 16,384 | 56.1M | −14% | −15% | −2% | −29…−30% |
| accounts, 65,536 | 206.3M | −16% | −17…−20% | −2% | −33…−36% |

- **Word-level memory.** The ledger takes a row per access instead of per
  byte, two per word first touched and closed, and the value as a word (+7
  columns; an unaligned access spanning two words and a sub-word write
  need more, so the width is light). The Poseidon2 chip's own memory
  traffic is not in the access count. The boundary takes a row per distinct word, between distinct
  bytes / 8 and / 4. The auth kernel's 626 accesses of 4,253 bytes, and
  its 1,189 witness bytes, put today's byte ledger at 2^14 rows. At word
  level it falls to 2^11. Wherever memory is the larger chip, this lever
  matches or beats the split. It also takes the memory family's byte
  columns (`MemByteActive` and three carries a byte, 32 columns) out of the
  CPU, which the table does not count.
- **Right shifts through MUL:** `x >> n` is the high word of `x · 2^(64−n)`
  for `n ≥ 1`, as the rotations already compute it (`n = 0` is a special
  case). The auth kernel's 44 logical right shifts are the only reason its
  shape holds DIVREM (413 columns at the floor, 7.2% of its cells), and
  the shifts it moves fit MUL's floor. At heavier lengths the table's −2%
  is an upper bound: it drops DIVREM without growing MUL (`accounts`' first
  2^16 steps take MUL from 2^12 to 2^13 rows, +2.4M cells against −3.4M),
  nor counts the CPU's quotient group it frees. No traced kernel divides,
  but `balance` and others shift arithmetically (`SharRImm64`), which keeps
  DIVREM unless that shift moves too, through MulUpper's sign correction.
  A narrow change to the shared chip set, with its own gates and a
  re-pin.
- **The floors.** At 1,722 steps, 4.25M of the auth kernel's 11.67M cells
  are chips held at the 2^10 floor for little work. The Poseidon2 chip
  takes 2.18M for 43 permutations, MUL 0.61M and DIVREM 0.85M for a few
  dozen rows. No CPU lever touches these. `h` sets the floor (format 45's
  `4h`); only a smaller `h`, or fewer chips, moves it.

## 6. Effort and risk [est]

| variant | what it takes | weeks | risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **per-program CPU** | The CPU's columns in optional groups (the ten witness groups and the absent opcodes' flags as blocks); a feature mask from the code (as `device_chips_of_code`), in the program id and the node's `Shape`; absent columns read as constant zero in `Row`; entries of an absent group skipped, the rest paired anew (every CPU tuple is degree 1, so any pairing holds the degree bound); the trace fill by block; `leaf.rs`'s CPU entry per mask. Gates: per group, a program without it whose trace runs one of its opcodes is refused (ProgramMemory's canonical flags against the constant zero); the mask is taken from every instruction in the program-memory table, unreachable ones and `Trap` included; the degree audit, the masking's `h` check, the pairing and the leaf entry per mask. A group's constraints that also serve present opcodes stay with the group's columns read as zero (the unsigned loads' zero extension in `signed_load_extension`, for one). Re-pin: a new device format (45's successor), 41's definition before 7.21, fixtures and constants. | 3–5 (optimistic: each mask its own pairing, audit and fixtures) | Low: no new relation; one component; the leaf narrows. The pairing per mask and the mask's place in the program id are the review's points. |
| 2-way split (ALU / rest) on top | A second CPU component; the family id in program memory (+1 preprocessed); the trap check restated; per-family trace fill and interaction. Gates: an ALU opcode run in the rest's component and the reverse, a step in both or in neither (the execution multiset), the trap's successor, per component and on a node segment with the boundary. | +2–3 | Medium: a new cross-component argument to review, though carried by an existing relation. Fits 41's leaf with the chip by 963 rows. |
| the plan's 4-way split | As above, four components, the host family separate. | 6–10 (device), 8–12 (both) | It overflows a node leaf with the Poseidon2 chip, so it is device-only (S32). It gains −5.4% on the auth kernel and −2…−24% at heavier lengths. |
| word-level memory (7.19) | As planned. | 4–6 | As planned; its sub-word gates. |
| right shifts through MUL | The CPU's shift rows' tuple routed to MUL; DivRem kept for division and the arithmetic shift, or that shift through MulUpper. | 1–2, more with the arithmetic shift | Low; format re-pin with the next one. |

The column counts are a model of a redesign, not of code. A redesign may
find more: family-specific flag bytes, or a narrower timestamp (23 columns
and 8 range checks a row today, for steps far below 2^24). It may also
find less: the family selector, and constraints the group rule misjudged.
Read them as ±10%.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Build the per-program CPU first**, for both formats. It gives −9% on
   the auth kernel and −14–17% on heavier kernels, 29–32% of the CPU's
   cells at every length. It narrows the node leaf (−1.3k `p2` rows), which
   folds also gain, and adds headroom to 41's count. It takes 3–5 weeks.
2. **Then word-level memory (7.19)** if PG2's margin or heavier device
   kernels matter. It is the bigger lever at the auth size (−17%), and with
   step 1, −26% (607 → about 450 ms).
3. **A 2-way split, if any, judged per kernel length.** With step 1 it adds
   −6% on the auth kernel (its two components at 874 and 848 of 1,024
   rows, so a few dozen more steps would erase it) and about −21 points of
   a kernel's CPU cells averaged over lengths above 2^10 (−32% → −53%),
   nothing at an exact power of two. Do not build the 4-way
   split. It is wider and jagged at powers of two. Over the full ISA it is
   device-only by S32. Over the per-program CPU it gains only 5–8 points of
   the CPU's cells more than the 2-way, with two more components to gate,
   and leaves a node leaf with the chip under 1% of headroom.
4. **Heavier circuits on phones** meet 1.0 s first (about 20M cells,
   2^11–2^12 steps of node-like code), then 1 GiB (26–40M cells). The
   levers above together (−30–45% at 2^12–2^14 steps) move those limits by
   under one octave, not to 2^16. Before relying on the peak's slope,
   measure one heavier kernel at format 45.
