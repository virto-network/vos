## 1. Soundness and privacy of the credential design

**1a. The AIR statements.** Auth (`proving.md:960-967`) proves
`leaf(member(s))` under `map_root` by 32 boolean-constrained selects and
`nullifier = C(C(T(nf), s), scope)`; owner (`:968-974`) proves
`owner = C(T(owner), s)`. Both are proofs of knowledge of `s` under
Poseidon2-M31's compression with domain tags, masked (zero knowledge from
§5's masking, two components so the claimed sums blind, `:990-993`). The
counts are right: 1 + 1 + 32 + 2 = 36, 1, and 37 for the joint circuit.
`T(name)` blocks `[t, 0, …, 0]` and `W(d)`'s eight 31-bit words
(`:914-920`) are unambiguous; `W` drops 8 of 256 bits, so a binding or scope
collision needs a second preimage of BLAKE2b differing only in those bits,
2^-248 a try. The slot is positional, not hashed (`:945-947`): right, a `u32`
would not fit a word, and the path's 32 bits place the leaf.

**One error an implementer would build literally (M8):** `proving.md:984-985`
says "`scope` and `binding` enter no hash in the circuit: ingress computes
both". `scope` *is* the second input of the nullifier's outer compression
(`:931`, `:966`); what ingress computes is `scope` *from its BLAKE2b
preimage*. As written, a circuit that treats `scope` as a free public word
yields a nullifier `C(T(nf), s)` alone: one pseudonym across every scope, the
global handle the owner refuses. Say: "neither is computed from its preimage
in the circuit; `scope` is an input of the nullifier's compression, `binding`
a public word only."

The binding as a public word that no gate reads is bound by the transcript
(mixed before the relations are drawn) and, as §8 says, by the public sums;
either suffices for "a proof for `b` fails under `b'`". 7.10's gate "a
changed binding refused" (`slice-7-mobile.md:829-831`) is the right test.
The program id is chosen by `kind`, never from the proof, and `b` names the
program (`identity-and-authority.md:272-280`), so a format-43 device proof
and a format-42 auth proof for one call have distinct `b`: right.

**1b. Nullifier scoping and `once`.** `scope` carries the `SpaceId`, agent,
instance and name under a domain tag (`:555-558`); the nullifier is `scope`
keyed by `C(T(nf), s)`, so two scopes' pseudonyms are unlinkable under the
usual PRF assumption on the keyed compression, and neither links to `member`
or `owner` (different tags). The `once` guard (`:298-306`) keeps the set in a
system row `once(scope ‖ nullifier) → t` scoped by construction; ingress
refuses on the replica's applied state, apply decides. Sound. **One gap
(M1):** apply's step 5 records the `once` row *before* the method runs
(`:363-367`), and step 6 says a `Forbidden` consumes the input with `b`
recorded. So a handler that fails (a ballot the vote handler refuses, a
trap) has spent the member's one admission for good; the design does not say
whether that is intended. `b` must stay recorded whatever happens (replay);
the `once` row should be recorded only when the handler ran and returned
`Ok` (a refused vote is not a cast vote), or the opposite stated. Owner
question Q2; one test either way.

**1c. Replay and `Replayed`.** `b` covers space, agent, instance, method,
program, kind, arguments, nonce and expiry with fixed widths and SCALE
lengths (`:271-280`): canonical. Ingress refuses `expires_ms` past 120 s,
apply refuses `expires_ms < t`, records `b` until `expires_ms + 60 s` by the
due index, and a refused input writes nothing (`:356-373`). A proof applied
once anywhere runs nowhere twice; after the sweep the binding cannot return
because apply refuses it by time. The intercepted-proof case is stated
honestly (`:600-604`: the same call, once). S39's `Replayed` without the
first outcome is fine; a client reads state. Two enums now carry `Replayed`
and `Once` at the same code 8 and 10/12 (`CredentialRefusal` at ingress,
refine's `Refusal` at apply): the text should say which it means where (L2).
The codes themselves are free: `Rejection` 0–8 built so `Credential` is 9;
refine's `Refusal` 1–6 built so 7–10 are free; `Input` 0–9 built so
`Credentialed` is 10; `Principal` 0–2 so 3, 4; `Failure` 1–14 so
`Credentials` is 15. All checked.

**1d. Freshness, revocation, `rekey` and `freeze`.** The ring's rule
(`:470-480`) runs from a root's supersession, so an honest path stays good
through unrelated enrollments and a revocation binds every replica within
`ROOT_MAX_AGE` of its batch (apply checks by batch time, `:359-360`): the
7.7 record's fix of the draft's rule is right. Within 10 minutes a revoked
member still authenticates; stated (`:605-609`). Payment reads no tree, so
revocation does not stop it; S37's `rekey(row, owner)` and `freeze(row)`
under an operator role do, and double as a lost key's recovery. That is a
custody power (an operator can move any row), which is the tier the owner
chose (S37: each space picks its privacy tier); the log attributes it to the
operator's origin, so it is visible, and §7 should say in one line that the
in-space ledger is custodial by design (L8 carries it).

**1e. Unlinkability across spaces; the handle vs the source's reference.**
`s = f(handle, o)` (`proving.md:1022-1026`): two handles give unrelated
secrets from one source; a copied handle shares `s`, hence `member` and
`owner`, and the scopes' `SpaceId` keeps nullifiers apart (`:1027-1031`,
`identity-and-authority.md:628-630`). The device keeps the source's
reference and the pinned handle, nothing derived. Right. One consequence
worth a sentence: because the HMAC key is one key for every space (the input
varies), a member cannot have two unrelated secrets in two spaces sharing a
handle; that is the linkage already stated.

**1f. The secret sources.** The Keystore HMAC under `BiometricPrompt` with
the `Mac` as crypto object, auth per use (`setUserAuthenticationParameters(0,
…)`, API 30 for device credential with a crypto object), not invalidated by
new enrollments (a PIN unlocks it anyway, so nothing is lost), StrongBox
when present: correct and buildable; 7.0b probes exactly the two things that
can fail. Containment is stated at its true size (`o` crosses into the
process; code there keeps `s` for good, `:568-586`). A lost key is a lost
`s`, remedied by revoke, re-enroll and `rekey`: consistent everywhere. **The
security key (L1):** CTAP2 `hmac-secret` returns HMAC(credRandomWithUV, salt)
or HMAC(credRandomWithoutUV, salt), two unrelated keys; the design's "with
its PIN and touch" must be "always with UV", or a use without the PIN
derives another `s`. And `makeCredential` still needs an `rpId` string (not
a WebAuthn relying party, no `assetlinks.json`): pin one constant and one
credential for every handle (the salt varies), its id the stored reference.

**1g. Path refresh and what still leaks.** `PathKeeper` fetches when
`root()` moves or on a jittered timer, never before a proof, with a key
drawn per query (`proving.md:1082-1099`); §7 says the leak is reduced, not
closed, and names the residue (`:610-621`): the address links a refresh's
digest to a proof's nullifier for the operators of the node and the gateway
(with a stable phone address the link is trivial), and `Anonymous` protects
against members and readers, not operators. That is the honest statement.
Two more residues to add to the same list: the `arguments` of a credentialed
call are in the log in clear (a payment's payee and amount beside the public
payer, which the design accepts), and a tree change makes every online
member refresh at once, so the set of online members is visible at each
enrollment. Syncing the tree is the later fix, as said.

## 2. The gateway and the node API (S41)

**2a. Authority-free, as far as the design goes.** Every effect on the node
is a signed call (ed25519 in a session), a signed query (`QueryCall { key,
not_after, …, signature }`), a proof (`Credentialed`), or an operator
request; the node runs every check whoever relays. The gateway can drop,
delay, reorder and read; it cannot forge. The mutual TLS allowlist is
*access*, not authority: a listed gateway may send frames, each still
verified. The leaks that remain are exactly the ones the implementer found:

- **Operator routes.** Today `POST /agents`, `/representative`,
  `/agents/{id}/{install,replicas,roles,reports}` are admitted by a bearer
  token in a header (`node/host/src/ingress.rs:200-290`). A gateway relaying
  it holds it, and holds every operator power. The proposal (signed by a
  listed operator key, nonce, expiry; `slice-7-mobile.md:855-858`) closes
  it; until the owner answers, the socket-only alternative (operator kinds
  refused over QUIC) is the safe default. Q1.
- **The operator console's home** (`:864`): a console *in* the gateway that
  signs for the operator would put the key back in the gateway. None is
  built now; 7.10b's text should say the console makes the operator's own
  client sign (L4).
- **`Time`.** Unsigned; a lying gateway makes a client pick an `expires_ms`
  the node refuses (a re-prove), and cannot extend a proof's life: ingress
  checks `now ≤ expires_ms ≤ now + 120 s` on its own clock. Say so in §7's
  threat model, or sign `Time` with the node key clients pin (L3).
- **Rate limits** before authentication can only be per address, so a
  hostile gateway censors; stated (drop, delay). **Leader routing** to a
  lagging replica cannot help an attacker: apply decides replay, `once` and
  freshness by batch time.
- **The s4 surface.** The ephemeral SSH key is "no one in particular"; the
  s4 server's `Identity` is the connection's and quotas, resume and grants
  hang off it (`server/README.md`, Limits; STATE-MACHINES, Resume: `resume:
  null` is already the ephemeral session). The gateway's s4 app holds no
  credential bytes (the facility builds them, the app forwards). One thing
  it *does* hold: whoever calls `claim(member)` and the ledger's row claim
  signs a VOS call, so the s4 app has a client key of its own; that is an
  ordinary client's authority bounded by the registrar's invitations, and
  should be said (M2).
- **Trusted-chrome confirmation.** The facility encoding the transfer's
  arguments from the two fields the chrome shows (`:964-969`) is the right
  mechanism against a document that says one thing and calls another, with
  one hole (M3): the server supplies the payee, and if the chrome shows an
  opaque row key the user cannot compare it with the document's "Bob". A
  hostile server document can still steer a payment to a payee the user does
  not recognize as wrong. The chrome must show something the user can judge:
  collect payee and amount in the facility's own form (the server's call a
  prefill at most), or key the demo's ledger by names shown verbatim. The
  same for `sign_in`: a server may send a `once`-guarded method's call as a
  "sign in" and burn the member's vote; the chrome should name the method
  and the guard. Grants and per-call confirmations are also conflated at
  `:985-988` (a grant is per session at `ServerHello`; the payee prompt is
  per call), L5.

**2b. The split itself.** Sound and overdue (one binary API, HTTP byte for
byte in a differential test, the node's HTTP deleted last). What 7.10b's
brief must fix at its start, since (S) means Fable reads it first: the frame
grammar (length and kind widths, the request id over the socket, the answer
frame, and a frame-level error for an oversize or unknown kind, which the
ingress table's row 1 calls "the node API's" without a name), and the kind
table with each kind's size limit (L9). And the dependency (M6, §3).

## 4. Implementable as written?

Pins G, I, K and the statements: yes; an implementer has every word, order
and code. The gaps that would cause rework are all on the demo's side and in
the library's wire helpers:

- **The client's transport for `path`, `root()` and `Time` (M2).** s4
  facilities are server-initiated (`STATE-MACHINES.md`, Facilities: `call →
  result | cancel`); a client cannot open a request of its own over the s4
  session, and the design wants the refresh "on a connection of its own,
  with a key of its own" (`slice-7-mobile.md:960-963`) while the phone
  reaches only the gateway's SSH port by `adb reverse` (`:978-980`). As
  written the implementer must invent the channel. Q4 gives the options; the
  cheapest is the gateway's HTTP port reverse-forwarded too, the library's
  `path_query`/`Time` bodies posted to `/agents/{id}/queries` and `/time`.
- **The ledger the demo needs (M4).** 7.11's test ledger (`:895-898`) has
  `transfer` under `Owner`, `rekey` and `freeze`; 7.13 also needs the row
  model (rows keyed by owner digest, `transfer(to, amount)` debiting the
  caller's row; or rows with ids and `from` an argument), the two-step
  `invite_row`/`claim_row` §6 promises (`identity-and-authority.md:489`), a
  `balance` query, and funding (`mint` under `operator`). Who signs
  `claim_row` and `claim` for a member with no key: the s4 app's client key
  under the registrar's invitation (§2a).
- **The payee the chrome shows (M3)**, §2a.
- **The client's ephemeral key (L6).** "What s4 changes" (`:981-988`) lists
  the server's anonymous policy; the client today uses one app-managed
  ed25519 identity and saves resume tokens and host entries
  (`client/README.md`, Connection shell). The client needs a per-connection
  key for the credential surface, no saved token and no remembered grants
  for it, the gateway's host-key pin kept.
- **Methods pinned (L11).** "A later request naming another space, ledger
  agent or method is refused" (`:968-969`) but `enroll` pins the handle, the
  space and the agents (`:945-947`); pin the method ids (or names resolved
  from the schema) at enrollment, or have the facility hard-code `transfer`
  and `login`.
- **Small ones:** `Threads::Auto` ships in 7.12 before 7.17's core-class
  weights, so a plain default first (L7); the facility needs API 30 while
  s4 runs from API 23, so it reports unsupported below (L7); the joint
  membership-and-payment circuit is a *floor* for a shielded spend (notes,
  values, ranges are far above 37 permutations), say so (L8).

## Owner questions

- **Q1. Operator requests over the node API** (the plan's open question 3).
  (a) Signed by an operator key the node's config lists, nonce and expiry
  as a call's; (b) the bearer token kept, carried in the frame; (c)
  operator kinds admitted over the unix socket only, refused over QUIC.
  **Recommend (a)**, with (c) as the remote gateway's default until (a) is
  built; (b) leaves every operator power in a relaying gateway.
- **Q2. The `once` row when the handler fails.** (a) Recorded only when the
  handler returned `Ok`, `b` recorded regardless; (b) recorded at admission
  whatever the handler does. **Recommend (a):** a refused ballot is not a
  cast vote, and a retry costs the member a new proof, so (a) opens no
  grinding.
- **Q3. The payee before an owner proof.** (a) The facility's own chrome
  form collects payee and amount, the server's `pay` call a prefill the user
  may edit; (b) the chrome shows the row key verbatim and the demo's ledger
  keys rows by human-readable names; (c) as written. **Recommend (a)** for
  7.13 (a few lines of chrome, the strongest guarantee); (b) acceptable;
  not (c).
- **Q4. The demo client's channel for `path`, `root()` and `Time`.** (a) The
  gateway's HTTP port, reverse-forwarded too; the library's bodies posted to
  `/agents/{id}/queries` and `/time` with a fresh key per query; (b) an SSH
  subsystem on the gateway (`vos-api`) tunnelling node-API frames under an
  ephemeral key, an s4 client extension; (c) the facility hands the query
  bodies to the server to forward, which gives up "a connection of its
  own". **Recommend (a)** now, (b) as the later clean path.
- **Q5. 7.10b's start.** (a) Now, beside 7.9 and 7.10, the node's HTTP
  deleted after 7.6's final run (or T1 re-measured over the node API and
  both recorded); (b) after 7.10 as planned. **Recommend (a):** 2–3 weeks
  off M4's chain, and the credentialed call is still born on the API.
- **Q6. The input's domain tag.** (a) `"vos/device/input/v1"`; (b) keep
  `"vos/prf/v1"`. **Recommend (a)**, before 7.12 pins a constant that
  names a mechanism the design dropped.

## Verdict

**Fix-then-merge.** Fold M8 and M1 (one sentence each plus a test), M5 and
M7 (the library's wire helpers and the tag), M6 (the dependency, the chain
and the estimate lines), and M2–M4 as the owner answers Q3–Q4; the lows at
will. The credential design is sound: the statements prove what the pins
say, the nullifier is scoped by construction, replay and revocation bind
every replica by batch time, and the privacy claims stop where the operators
begin. The gateway is authority-free once operator requests are signed; the
demo's remaining risks are in what the chrome shows and how the client
reaches the node around the s4 session, both decidable now.
